Preface

These twelve stories continue the collection Cargoes and Legacies: the same universe, the same people — a few years further on. Admiral Herald inherits a fleet and a book of rules; Simone Vorkosigan inherits a beret and a habit; a singer, a kin of mantids, a pilot-cartographer and an archivist inherit more awkward things — questions; a child on Rho Ceta inherits a catalogue with an impossible specimen in it. Lady Ekaterin inherits the heaviest thing of all: clean earth, and the decision of what shall grow on it. The Emperor and his Auditor inherit what empires never learn to hand down — an apology without words — and then, in ten chapters for a tenth sarcophagus, a secret that is permitted to remain unknown. And at the last the Imperium does the unthinkable: it sits down before a Betan microphone, live, and discovers that its finest weapon has been living on Sergyar all along and drinks its tea unsedated.

The stories stand alone, but they answer one another: on inheriting a command, on the freedom of the made thing, on the reading of trails — and on coming home.
